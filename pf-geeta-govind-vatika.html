The project

Nineteen acres in Taj Nagri Phase-II, built and fitted out by ADA, now looking for an operator. The asset is already there: musical fountains, a 40-minute Krishna Leela laser show, an open-air amphitheatre, a waterbody, eight kiosks, a Tulsi forest of over a hundred species, and thematic sculpture and landscaping across the site. The operator pays a monthly licence fee and keeps what it collects at the gate.

What the Agra cluster does to the commercialsGeeta Govind Vatika sits next to Agra Chaupati and a short drive from Subhash Park, both already operating. Three consequences run straight into the numbers in this deck. Overhead is charged at 5% of revenue rather than a standalone rate, because the Agra management line, maintenance crew and hiring pipeline are already funded. Marketing reaches an audience already visiting the cluster, which is what makes a volume-led opening season realistic rather than optimistic.

Why year one funds itselfThree decisions, taken together, make the first year cash-positive rather than a hole to be financed. Programme early. The year-1 plan is a marketing year: public events and E-O-D-run IPs that bring people through the gate at ADA's ₹20 price rather than a premium one — ₹30 L of event revenue in year 1 against a standing start. Price for volume. Footfall is modelled at 3.75 lakh visits in year 1 — about 1,027 a day — on reasonable pricing, not on squeezing the ticket. Borrow the kitchen. The food stalls are supplied out of E-O-D's existing shops at Agra Chaupati, five minutes away.
The Agra Chaupati supply line is worth two separate thingsIt cuts the capex. Geeta Govind Vatika needs counters, shade and seating — not a built kitchen. The kiosk line in the schedule above is ₹8 L, against the ₹18 lakh a built-out F&B kitchen would have cost. That difference is most of what brings the ask down to a round crore.

It cuts the cost of goods. Product moves from EAC's kitchens at transfer cost rather than being bought in from a third party, so F&B runs at 44% cost of goods rather than the 52% a bought-in operation carries. On year-5 F&B revenue that is worth about ₹5.9 L a year, every year, for no additional investment.
On the licence fee bidThis model assumes a winning bid of ₹36 L a year — ₹3.0 lakh a month, 20% above ADA's ₹2.5 lakh reserve. Solving the model for the licence fee at which the project IRR falls to its own 12.10% cost of guaranteed debt gives ₹80.9 L a year — ₹6.74 L a month, or roughly 2.7× the reserve. That is real headroom in the auction room, and it is a consequence of the year-one plan: a project that earns from month one can afford to bid harder than one that needs a year of borrowed opex first.

Secure written agreement on the show tariff and its escalation path before the auction, not after the work order.

Verdict
Equity-fundable
Project IRR 43.3% against an all-in CGTMSE cost of debt of 12.10% (+31.2 percentage points) and an equity hurdle of 22%. Equity-fundable: the project out-earns a private-equity hurdle.
How the exit is valuedA licence that expires is a wasting asset. The EBITDA-multiple cross-check at 4.5× would give ₹5.97 Cr — higher, and in our view not defensible for an asset with two years left to run at that point.

The facility fits — but only just, and only with the moratoriumThe project can service ₹1.62 Cr at a 1.30× floor. The facility as briefed is ₹1.00 Cr. There is ₹0.62 Cr of headroom and no more. The three-year principal moratorium is doing the work: it pushes the first repayment into year 4, when EBITDA is ₹1.06 Cr rather than ₹0.51 Cr. A bank that offers this facility with a one-year moratorium is offering a facility that defaults in year 2.
Do not over-sanction the working-capital limitCGTMSE charges its guarantee fee on the sanctioned limit in year 1 and on the outstanding balance thereafter. A ₹0.20 Cr limit that peaks at ₹0.01 Cr of actual drawdown is paying a guarantee fee on air. Cutting the sanctioned limit to ₹0.20 Cr and keeping the balance as an uncommitted overdraft saves ₹0.00 L of guarantee fee over the term and consumes ₹0.00 Cr less of the group's ₹10 Cr CGTMSE ceiling — which, as the portfolio hub sets out, is the binding constraint across all three projects.

Option C · Debt converted to equity

A compulsorily convertible debenture: money in as debt, a coupon while the project ramps, then automatic conversion into equity on a formula fixed the day it is issued.

Worse than either pure option, at project levelThe CCD returns 11.7% — below the equity case, because the coupon years consume the cash the project needs while it is ramping, and the conversion stake is not large enough to make up the difference on exit. At project level a CCD combines the cash-flow burden of debt with the dilution of equity and the guarantee benefit of neither. It has a real place at company level — see the company deck — but not here.
If a CCD is used anywhere in this structure, three things must be trueOne: the conversion formula must be fixed on the date of issue — a price determined later fails the FEMA pricing test if any part of the money is foreign, and turns the instrument into external commercial borrowing. Two: a valuation report under section 62(1)(c) of the Companies Act is needed before conversion, and the issue itself is a private placement under section 42 with the filings that follow. Three: until it converts, a CCD sits on the balance sheet as borrowing — it worsens the debt-to-equity ratio that any parallel CGTMSE facility will be covenanted on.

12

Risks and open items

Auction overshoot
High
The forward e-auction has no cap and the licence fee escalates 5% a year on whatever is bid. Every ₹1 lakh a year above the modelled ₹36 L costs about ₹8.1 lakh across the seven-year term. Mitigation: a board-approved walk-away of ₹80.9 L a year, set before the auction opens.
ADA tariff control
High
ADA fixes the gate at ₹20 and approves every other rate, with the right to revise. The show ticket carries roughly a third of the revenue and E-O-D cannot price it. Mitigation: agree the show tariff and its escalation path in writing before the work order, not after.
Laser and fountain asset condition
Medium–High
The agency inherits equipment of unknown age and must replace anything that reaches end of life at its own cost, with the onus of proving irreparability on the agency. Mitigation: a joint condition survey and the AMC history before bid submission; price the risk into the auction limit.
Year-1 cash
Medium
The project loses ₹-0.17 Cr at EBITDA in year 1 while paying six months of licence fee in advance. Mitigation: this is precisely what the working-capital limit is for; the three-year principal moratorium keeps debt service out of the ramp.
No permanent structures
Medium
Clause 3.3.2 bars permanent construction. The activity layer — E-O-D's core margin driver — must be demountable, which raises unit cost and shortens asset life. Mitigation: the capex schedule assumes an eight-year life on demountable equipment, not fifteen.
Concession renewal
Medium
Seven years, extendable by four at ADA's discretion, with the licence fee revisable for the extended term. Mitigation: no extension value is included in any return in this deck. E-O-D holds two existing ADA concessions with a clean record.
Electricity arrears
Medium
Electricity is paid direct to the discom monthly, and VAPPL carries ₹2.14 Cr of accumulated arrears across the existing estate. A new site inherits scrutiny. Mitigation: clear the arrears before any lender sanction — it is a condition precedent in the company deck as well.
Open items before submission
