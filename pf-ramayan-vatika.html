Two clauses that change how this can be financedNo charge over the asset. “The Vatika, wholly or partially, shall not be mortgaged, pledged, hypothecated or offered as security to any bank, FI, NBFC or third party.” The Vatika cannot secure anything. That removes conventional project finance entirely and leaves a collateral-free CGTMSE facility as the only sensible debt route — the guarantee substitutes for the security the RFP forbids.

No change of control without consent. “No change in ownership pattern, shareholding structure or controlling interest during the lock-in without BDA's prior written approval.” Any equity issue that shifts VAPPL's shareholding, and any convertible instrument at the moment it converts, needs BDA's prior written approval during the lock-in. This is a condition precedent on Options A and C, not a footnote.

The performance security is the hidden cost₹30 L sits with BDA, interest-free, for the entire fifteen-year term. At a 11.5% opportunity cost that is roughly ₹3.45 lakh a year of foregone return, or about ₹52 lakh across the contract — more than one and a half years of licence fee. It is real, it is not recoverable, and it should be priced into the bid rather than treated as a refundable deposit.
Sizing the facility against what the project can carryA lender will size on debt service coverage, not on the borrower's stated need. On the base case this project services ₹2.81 Cr — a term loan of ₹1.55 Cr and a working-capital limit of ₹1.26 Cr — at a 1.30× floor. The recommended structure in section 11 therefore sanctions the full ₹4.17 Cr as a limit, commits ₹2.81 Cr of it, and leaves the balance available but uncommitted. Expected peak drawdown is lower still, at ₹2.36 Cr.

The manpower floor is contractual, not discretionaryBDA prescribes a minimum deployment: 4 sweepers and 5 security personnel per shift, plus 8 gardeners, a site manager, an electrician and a plumber per day — around 29 heads before a single ticket is sold. Add ticketing, show technicians and food-court staff and the operating establishment is roughly 37 people. EPF and ESI are mandatory and explicitly called out. Payroll is the largest cost line in every year of this contract and it cannot be flexed down in a bad season. That is the structural reason the margin here is thinner than at Geeta Govind Vatika.
Restoration risk at handoverThe operator must return every asset “in the same condition as recorded at the time of initial handover, without any reduction in value, quality or serviceability”, with BDA's decision final and binding, and a pre-handover inspection thirty days out that can direct further works at the operator's cost. A 51-foot bronze and a Miyawaki forest are not assets whose fifteen-year condition can be underwritten confidently. Mitigation: a detailed, photographed, jointly signed asset handover report on day one, and a restoration provision accrued annually rather than met from year-15 cash.

Verdict
Does not clear its own cost of capital
Project IRR 11.4% against an all-in CGTMSE cost of debt of 12.50% (-1.1 percentage points) and an equity hurdle of 22%. Below the cost of debt. Do not gear this project as modelled — re-bid the licence fee, renegotiate the manpower schedule, or stand down. The gap is narrow, not catastrophic, and it closes entirely on BDA's own revenue assumption — see section 07. But it does not close by hoping. It closes by bidding at the reserve price and by getting the show economics confirmed in writing before submission.
Payback lands after the lock-in expires — but only justFree cash flow turns cumulatively positive in year 7.0. The contractual lock-in runs five years, during which exit means paying out the remaining contractual amount for the unexpired period. There is no cheap way out of a bad outcome here. On a seven-year reading of the lock-in — clause 9 of the Key Terms says seven where clause 14 says five — the position is worse still. Resolve that conflict by pre-bid query before anything is committed.

09

Option B · Debt under CGTMSE

The RFP forbids any charge over the Vatika. That is not an obstacle to a CGTMSE facility — it is the reason to use one. The guarantee replaces the security the contract will not allow.

Why the guarantee is structurally the right answer here“The Vatika, wholly or partially, shall not be mortgaged, pledged, hypothecated or offered as security to any bank, FI, NBFC or third party.” A conventional lender secures a project loan on the project. Here there is nothing to secure it on: BDA owns the land, the statue, the show and every fixture, and expressly bars them being pledged. CGTMSE covers a facility with nil collateral and no third-party guarantee, taking only hypothecation of the assets the borrower itself finances — ticketing systems, food-court equipment, the toy train. Personal guarantees of directors remain permissible. The scheme and the contract fit each other exactly.

The facility as briefed does not clear a bank's coverage testAt ₹4.17 Cr the minimum post-moratorium DSCR is 1.09×. Banks underwrite CGTMSE term facilities to 1.30× and prefer 1.50×. The project services ₹2.81 Cr at that floor. The workable structure is therefore a sanctioned limit of ₹4.17 Cr with a committed, drawn component of ₹2.81 Cr and the balance uncommitted — or a smaller facility with the difference met from group cash. Presenting the full two years of opex to a credit committee as committed term money will get the proposal declined.
Right-size the sanctioned limitThe guarantee fee is charged on what is sanctioned, not what is used. Trimming the working-capital limit from ₹2.27 Cr to ₹1.70 Cr saves ₹0.57 L of guarantee fee and frees ₹0.57 Cr of the group's ₹10 crore CGTMSE ceiling.

Three separate reasons this is the worst of the three hereCash. The coupon falls due in years 1 to 4 — precisely the years the project is losing money at EBITDA. ₹0.75 Cr has to come from somewhere, and it is not coming from the gate.

Consent. Conversion changes the shareholding pattern, which needs BDA's prior written approval inside the lock-in. An instrument that must convert on a fixed date, into a shareholding change that a third party can refuse to approve, is a structural mismatch. If BDA withholds consent the instrument cannot perform.

Leverage. Until it converts a CCD is borrowing. Layered on VAPPL's existing 3.04× debt-to-equity it breaches the gearing covenant on any CGTMSE facility running alongside it.
If a convertible is used, use it at company levelThe company deck sets out a ₹12 crore CCD at VAPPL level returning 21.2% to the investor while deferring dilution — a genuinely competitive structure. The instrument is sound; this is the wrong project to attach it to.

Recommendation
Bid at reserve. Fund with a CGTMSE limit, drawn to ₹2.81 Cr.
Of the three structures only debt is both viable and permitted without BDA's consent — and it is viable only if the facility is sanctioned at ₹4.17 Cr but committed at ₹2.81 Cr, which is what the project services at a 1.30× coverage floor. The binding condition sits earlier than the financing, though. At BDA's ₹30 L reserve the project already returns 11.4% against a 12.50% cost of guaranteed debt — it does not cover the money that funds it before a single rupee of premium is bid. Every rupee above reserve comes straight out of that gap, and it escalates for the full term. Bid at or barely above reserve, or do not bid. On a contract with a five-year lock-in and a fifteen-year restoration obligation, winning at the wrong price is worse than losing.
What would change this recommendationWritten confirmation from BDA on three points would move this project from marginal to clearly fundable: (i) the show's capacity, daily slot count and any soft-launch footfall data, which is what the conversion assumption turns on; (ii) agreement on the show tariff and a contractual escalation path, rather than annual discretion; (iii) resolution of the five-versus-seven-year lock-in conflict. All three are legitimate pre-bid queries and all three are free to ask.

Risks and open items

Show conversion
High
