Investor Q&A — Objection Handling

E-O-D Adventure Parks · Vision Amusement Park Pvt. Ltd. · First in-person meeting, post virtual presentation · May 2026
How to use this. Don't wait to be asked. The three "narrative landmines" below are things a sharp investor will find in the numbers — own them first and they become evidence of candour. For every objection: acknowledge the fact, reframe it, land on a number. Anchor the room to EBITDA, not PAT (FY25-26 depreciation is still a placeholder pending the August 2026 audit).
A · Narrative landmines — raise these yourself
"Your revenue actually fell 14% in FY24-25." High
Acknowledge
Yes — ₹14.99 Cr → ₹12.82 Cr. We don't hide it; it's on the trajectory chart. Reframe
It was a sector-wide down year — Wonderla revenue −4.6%, PVR INOX box office −9%. Our steeper dip reflects that headwind plus two company-specific events: a heavier-than-usual monsoon hit EMV when it didn't yet have enough covered-activity capacity, while senior bandwidth was absorbed launching DME in a new city.
The fix worked: Feb-2025 capex (indoor Laser Tag, covered Archery, upgraded Rain Dance) ran its first full season in FY25-26 and drove the +27% rebound to ₹16.29 Cr — above the sector's recovery. The dip diagnosed a product gap; we closed it and grew through it.
"DME is loss-making. Why fund more highway parks?" Medium
Acknowledge
DME lost ₹37.51L on ₹25.42L revenue in Year 1. Reframe — it's a fixed-cost story, not a performance story
₹29.9L of that loss is fixed CAM paid to the sub-lessor — a structural lease cost independent of revenue — against just ₹25.4L from a product that wasn't finished (indoor-only; outdoor zone not yet built).
The raise-funded outdoor layer (Go Kart, Zipline, Obstacle Course; commissions Jan–Feb 2027) converts DME into a ₹2.5–3 Cr highway destination by FY27-28 — CAM then absorbs at ~10% of revenue. Karnal (signed, NH-1) replicates the completed format on India's busiest national highway, not the half-built one.
"What is your real net profit?" PAT only
Acknowledge & pre-empt
The provisional FY25-26 P&L carries a depreciation placeholder of ₹54.5L — that is the opening block depreciated on WDV, with the ₹3.68 Cr of FY26 additions not yet loaded into the fixed asset register. We have drafted the full asset-by-asset schedule under Schedule II of the Companies Act (in §06 of the financial model); it comes to ₹1.05 Cr. So we present PAT as a range (~₹0.66–1.37 Cr) and ask you not to anchor to a single figure until the August 2026 audit.
EBITDA is completely unaffected — ₹2.37 Cr, 14.5% margin. Depreciation is a non-cash timing item; it moves PAT, not the cash the business generates or the operating quality of the parks. Judge us on EBITDA and on EMV's 32.5% mature net margin.
B · The risk register — prepared answers
Concession renewal — the whole moat sits on government land. Med–High
This is the question that decides the deal. Mitigation, in order of weight:
  • Track record is the real mitigation — 10 years, zero incidents, 32.5% mature margins. Governments want E-O-D to succeed; our success proves their PPP model works.
  • No credible alternative operator exists in this category — switching cost for the body is extremely high, switching benefit near zero.
  • Two separate bodies (DTTDC for EMV; ADA for EAC + ESP) — no single-point failure.
  • Continuous formal written communication + personal relationships with senior officials (dedicated team under Apoorv Babbar).
  • Swadesh Darshan 2.0 registration across 4 states — a company under concession stress doesn't get invited into new government schemes.
Be ready to go deeper than the slide: have the actual concession terms, years remaining, and renewal history for EMV (DTTDC) and the ADA parks at your fingertips. If you can't answer the dates cold, this is the one place the story wobbles. Prep this before the meeting.
Revenue concentration — EMV is ~73% of revenue. High (now)
True today, and shrinking by design. The 3 new parks + Karnal reduce EMV's share to ~50% by FY28-29, with the Agra portfolio (EAC + ESP) building an independent revenue base in a second city. Concentration is a function of EMV being years ahead on the ramp curve — not fragility.
"Will EAC's ₹400 ticket hold before Phase 2 is built?" Medium
₹400 launches 22 May 2026 on existing activities — it is not raise-dependent. The justification is already there: EAC delivered a 30.7% margin at ₹200, which says the current activity stack is significantly under-priced. Phase 2 (live Jun/Jul 2027) is the retention accelerant, not the price justification.
This is also your strongest credibility card: near-term FY26-27 growth (₹23–25 Cr) does not depend on the investor's money. The raise accelerates FY27-28+, it doesn't rescue the base.
Debt load — ₹7.85 Cr borrowings, ₹71L finance costs. Medium
EBITDA interest coverage is already 4.7× (FY26). ₹5.26 Cr is short-term (13 bank/NBFC facilities), ₹2.60 Cr long-term related-party. The raise earmarks ₹2–3 Cr for NBFC repayment, cutting annual finance cost ~₹30–40L from FY27 and lifting coverage to 5×+ by FY28.
Zipline AMC — ₹1.11 Cr is 6.8% of FY26 revenue. Medium
Zipline is EMV's top-draw attraction. At ₹18 Cr EMV revenue (FY26-27) the AMC ratio improves to 6.2%. Ride-attributed revenue tracking is being formalised to evidence the ₹2 Cr+ it pulls. Honest framing: it's a premium-attraction cost that scales down as revenue grows.
Fundraise delay — does the model break if close slips? Medium
No. The base case already assumes a conservative Jan/Feb 2027 outdoor start (only ~2 months in FY26-27), and EAC ₹400 is raise-independent. Each 3-month slip costs ~₹60–80L of DME outdoor contribution — a deferral, not a structural hit. See downside scenario below: even with a 6-month delay, FY26-27 still lands ₹20–22 Cr / PAT ₹1.7–2.1 Cr.

D · Likely valuation pushback
"₹90 Cr on ₹16 Cr revenue is rich — that's 5.5×." Medium
Three lenses, all defensible, all at a discount to listed peers (6.5–7×):
  • Trailing 5.5× — but on a suppressed base: 3 of 4 parks were partial/first-year, contributing only ₹1.73 Cr against EMV's ₹14.56 Cr.
  • FY26-27 forward 3.8× (₹24 Cr) — EAC ₹400 from May, no raise dependency.
  • FY27-28 operational forward 2.7× (₹33 Cr) — first full year of DME outdoor + EAC Phase 2 — a ~60% discount to the peer group.
Closest comps: Nicco Parks (govt-JV land, same concession logic, ~6.5×) and Silverstorm (BSE SME DRHP, ₹31 Cr rev, 53% EBITDA — same listing route we'd pursue). Don't anchor on trailing EBITDA multiple — consolidated margin is depressed by three parks in ramp-up; EMV at maturity is 32.5% net.
